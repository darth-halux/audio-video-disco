One of the things that inspired me to create this website was watching the 1999 movie **10 Things I Hate About You**. It's not a deeply complex movie or anything, but I still had some thoughts after watching it, and I felt the lack of somewhere to register them. This was actually about 2 weeks ago, so the movie is not as fresh in my memory as it once was, but hopefully I can still put together something half coherent.

This movie is initially about a kid named Cameron, who moves into a fancy new high school and is immediately smitten by the local pretty girl Bianca Stratford. He finds out, however, that Bianca is forbidden from dating by her strict father until she either finishes high school OR her twin sister Kat begins dating as well. The latter being relevant because Kat is a recluse and seen as rude and generally a social outcast. Cameron then seeks the help of infamous bad boy Patrick, tasking him with wooing Kat so that Bianca is allowed to date.

So the first thing about this movie that caught my attention was how much of a quintessential turn-of-the-century high school romcom it is. It hits many of the clichés you'd expect: 

- The dorky but adorable guy gets the pretty girl;
- A goofy, clumsy side character that is unflinchingly loyal and helpful to the main character;
- "The prom" as a major plot point in the last leg of the movie;
- The main couple breaks up due to a misunderstanding, but later reconcile for a happy ending.

What sets it apart is that while the story is initially about Cameron seeking Bianca's affection, it quickly becomes more about Patrick and his (often unsuccessful) efforts to impress Kat. He initially does it for money, but eventually develops feelings for Kat, so it turns into this very nice and cute story about a bad boy having his heart softened and in turn softening a bad goth girl. To be clear, I think it's a good movie. It's very fun and well-paced, and the two "true" main characters were very well played by Julia Stiles and Heath Ledger. Yes that's the Joker! He speaks with an Australian accent in this movie too.

But what also impressed me was how 1999 the movie is. I guess it's one of those things where you forget that it's actually 27 years old, and that therefore it does make sense that it portrays a different society than the one we now live in. For example, a high school without phones seems to alien to me. I'm part of the first generation that had smartphones throughout high school, and one of the consequences of that is that we were never bored. It's an interesting difference, how in the movie there were plenty of scenes where one of the characters would be bored or trying to kill time with some activity, compared to how in school we would just pull out our phones and open social media or some game. There's also the clothes, the music, and (I don't want to be glib) a general pre-9/11 relaxed vibe about the whole movie. 

The part that irked me about the movie is how Kat's development is portrayed. At first, she's independent, confident and a self-proclaimed feminist. She is proud to say that dating is nowhere near the top of her list of priorities. Then Pat shows up, and while he fails at first, with some relatively surface level manipulation he manages to change her mind. To be specific, Bianca snoops around her sister's room to figure out her interests, and Pat kinda off-handedly mentioning these interests near Kat at a concert is enough to change her mind. Now, after this he does do something pretty nice, which is take care of Kat after she gets drunk at a party, but what makes Kat really fall for him is when he sings "Can't Take My Eyes Off You" using the school speaker system. To me this is weird: it feels out of character for someone like Kat to be won over by such a grandiose romantic gesture. This is me reading too much into it, but the underlying message feels like "Yeah she was kind of a stuck up feminist, but that was before she met this guy who REALLY gets her". Being alone and rejecting dating was presented as a flaw, something that had to be fixed, and Patrick is the (admittedly unconventional) knight in shining armor that comes to the rescue. I mean, on the surface it's sweet. That's why the movie was so successful. But it irked me a little.

The other weird part is Cameron. To give some more detail, Bianca was actually not initially interested in Cameron, and was using his efforts with Patrick to allow her to date a jock whose name I forgot. Later she realizes that Cameron was a Nice Guy after all, and they end the movie happily dating. Isn't that nice. Well, looking through a more critical lens, Cameron's actions are pretty reprehensible for someone who was initially the protagonist. When he first sees Bianca, he is warned by his comedic relief sidekick that she is a pretty but very superficial person; he ignores this advice and pursues her despite only getting further evidence that she is indeed superficial. In this pursuit he manipulates several people, including his crush's sister, and then acts all bummed out when he finds out he was being manipulated himself, even though that is only the cherry on top of the pile of evidence showing that Bianca is pretty on the outside, but kinda ugly inside.

But it gets worse!

Patrick sees him being all puppy dog eyes and tells him to not give up, since he should never let anyone stop him from getting what he wants. Damn what the hell. That's pretty absurd advice, especially in the context of romantic relationships. This is the only flaw in Patrick's character as far as I'm concerned by the way, it's this line. He's otherwise pretty awesome. And handsome. Anyway, because of this, Cameron rants to Bianca about how selfish she is (many of the things he criticizes her for here could be reflected back at him), then she realizes he's awesome and kisses him, and they kinda get an early happy ending. This is where they kinda fade into the background. Neither of them apologize to any of the people they manipulated, not even to Kat, who is the one most affected by their actions. There's a particularly egregious scene after the prom, where Cameron sees Kat all sad and dejected when he comes to their house to pick Bianca up. Instead of realizing that his reckless simping for a girl had significant consequences on others, and apologizing for it, he just kinda awkwardly greets her and then later asks Bianca "lol what's up with her?" to which she replies "eh idk she's weird". I think what I'm trying to get at is that Cameron kinda sucks, Bianca kinda sucks, and maybe it's right that they end up with each other.

Well, that's about all that I had to say. Again, overall the movie is great, and I'd watch it again. I think it's a good example of "clichés aren't bad if you simply do them right", and the criticisms I have are more so observations about the underlying messages that a story can tell, even if not on purpose. Honestly I feel a bit silly writing so much about a teenage romcom, but that's all, watch "10 Things I Hate About You" if you haven't!
